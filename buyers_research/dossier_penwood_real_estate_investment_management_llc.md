# Penwood Real Estate Investment Management, LLC - NJ industrial buyer dossier
*Prepared 2026-10-11 from buyers_research (A/B/C files) + 7 gap-filling searches. Confidence: med overall.*

## 1. Who they are and what they do in NJ
Penwood is a closed-end private-equity fund manager (SEC-registered adviser since 2005, ~18 staff, ~$1.5B regulatory AUM) based at 75 Isham Road, West Hartford, CT; no NJ office found in public sources (pack: https://fintrx.com/firms/firm/penwood-real-estate-investment-management-llc-130289). Its capital is pension money in commingled value-add funds: Fund VI closed 2021 at $325M; Partners VII closed 2023-09 at $425M with Connecticut, Virginia and Wisconsin retirement systems as LPs (https://irei.com/news/penwood-announces-final-close-for-penwood-select-industrial-partners-vii-fund/; https://realassets.ipe.com/news/penwood-raises-425m-for-new-value-add-industrial-fund/10069386.article). Active in NJ since at least 2018-2019, in 2022-2023 it bought three existing warehouses of 97,000-276,000 SF in the Meadowlands (Moonachie, Carlstadt) and at Turnpike Exit 7 (Bordentown), all through Fund VI (pack deal rows). It also converts non-industrial sites with local JV partners: retail-to-warehouse in Hamilton with Metrix, office-to-warehouse in Parsippany with Saxum (https://re-nj.com/saxum-penwood-eye-new-warehouse-project-in-parsippany-after-buying-vacant-office-building/). Strategy is buy/build-lease-sell: it sold the finished Hamilton building to Principal and an 83,220 SF Linden building to Link in 2019 (pack: https://re-nj.com/cw-penwood-metrix-sell-171000-sq-ft-hamilton-warehouse-after-redevelopment/; https://penwoodre.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/East-Linden-Sale-Press-Release.pdf).

## 4. Typical check size and deal shape
Priced building deals ran $31.25M-$60.0M ($218-$323/SF) for 97,000-276,000 SF; the stated floor is $10M and the Parsippany land JV was ~$16.3M (secondhand). Every NJ deal was single-asset through a fund vehicle with JLL-arranged bank acquisition debt at roughly 60-67% of price ($40M on $60M Bordentown). It buys vacant buildings and non-industrial sites, and develops only in JV with local operators (Metrix, Saxum). No NJ 1031, portfolio or sale-leaseback execution found in public sources.

## 7. What would make a property match them
- Existing warehouse/distribution/manufacturing/cold building, roughly 90,000-300,000 SF, Meadowlands or Turnpike corridor (Exits 7-13A); PA also in scope.
- Value-add story: vacant, short WALT or below-market rents; low coverage, heavy power or excess land a plus.
- Price $10M+; evidence puts the sweet spot at $25M-$75M, stated ceiling $100M+.
- Redevelopment: 10-30 acre obsolete office/retail/institutional sites that can go industrial, ideally with a local JV developer.
- Sale-leaseback or corporate-surplus sellers, single asset or small portfolio.
- Timing: Partners VII (2023 vintage) should be deploying through 2026-2027; expect bank-financed closings, not all-cash.

## 8. Five questions for the first call
1. How much of Partners VII's $425M is still uncommitted, and what hold period and exit year are you underwriting for a 2026 NJ buy?
2. After buying Carlstadt vacant, what vacancy or WALT will you take today, and are IOS, cold storage or manufacturing conversions in or out?
3. Which NJ submarkets are you prioritizing for 2026-2027 beyond the Meadowlands and Exit 7 - Exit 8A, the Port, or eastern PA?
4. Who approves a deal (Flynn, MacDonald, investment committee), and what is your typical LOI-to-close timeline and deposit structure given the use of acquisition debt?
5. Do you source through brokers (C&W, JLL, McBride, Chaus) or JV developers like Saxum and Metrix, and who should see an off-market NJ deal first?
