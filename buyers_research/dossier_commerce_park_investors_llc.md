# Commerce Park Investors LLC - NJ industrial buyer dossier
*Prepared 2026-10-11 from buyers_research (A/B/C files) + 7 gap-filling searches. Confidence: med overall.*

## 1. Who they are and what they do in NJ
Commerce Park Investors LLC (CPI) is a private, family-run developer-holder in East Hanover, NJ (72 Deforest Ave; a second site lists 15 West St) with no institutional parent; equity comes from the principals plus a sister syndication platform, Commerce Park Ventures, which says it recycles rent from its industrial and retail holdings into "undervalued, value-add assets" in New Jersey and Pennsylvania (pack: https://commerceparkventures.com/; https://commerceparkventures.com/investments/). CEO Spencer Pascal entered industrial in 2019 with a first warehouse in Roxbury (https://www.commerceparknj.com/our-team/spencer-pascal/), and his profile claims 5 industrial buys totaling 288,000+ SF in 2025 and a portfolio above 1.7M SF (https://www.linkedin.com/in/spencer-r-pascal/). What they actually buy is older small/mid-bay flex and heavy-power manufacturing, 12,000-53,000 SF, centred on Morris County (Roxbury, Randolph, Rockaway, Whippany), with 2025-2026 JV reach into Passaic, Bergen and Union counties (pack deal rows; section 3). The playbook is value-add and hold: renovate, split into small-bay or condo units, lease up through Resource Realty of Northern NJ (RRNNJ); some JV assets are flipped fast - Totowa was bought 2026-02 and resold 2026-10 (https://www.roi-nj.com/2026/10/09/real_estate_development/legacy-real-estate-developers-completes-sale-of-totowa-industrial-property-to-food-manufacturer/).

## 2. Stated buy box
No published criteria - pattern inferred from deals:
- Only public statements are generic: Commerce Park Ventures aims "to accelerate capital growth" by "funneling [rental cash flow] back into the acquisition of undervalued, value-add assets" in NJ and PA (https://commerceparkventures.com/investments/); CPI cites "disciplined underwriting, conservative capital structuring, and active asset management" (https://www.commerceparknj.com/).
- INFERRED size: 12,000-55,000 SF solo; up to ~125,000 SF with JV partners or debt.
- INFERRED price: $2M-$15M (priced deals $1.9M and $4.5M; $10M acquisition loan on Whippany).
- INFERRED product: 1950s-60s flex/manufacturing with heavy power, tailboard docks, drive-ins, divisible into 2,600-5,000 SF units; vacant or half-empty is acceptable.
- INFERRED geography: Morris County core; Passaic, Bergen, Union opportunistically via Legacy JVs.
- INFERRED structure: all-cash when JV'd with Legacy; bank debt on solo deals.

## 4. Typical check size and deal shape
Known prices are $1.9M and $4.5M ($54-$161/SF) for solo buys of 28,000-35,000 SF; Whippany carried a $10M acquisition loan, so that check was larger (sources above). Solo deals are single-asset (or two adjacent buildings) and bank-financed; the 2025-2026 JVs with Legacy/Saddleback were all-cash and ran 11,700-126,000 SF. Speed signals: three JV closings in 2026-07 (Linden was Legacy's "3rd property acquired in 24 hours", https://www.citybiz.co/article/875945/legacy-real-estate-developers-acquires-prime-linden-industrial-property/), one repeat buy-side broker (RRNNJ), and comfort with vacant or 48%-occupied stock. No 1031 or sale-leaseback evidence found.

## 7. What would make a property match them
- 10,000-60,000 SF freestanding industrial/flex; up to ~125,000 SF multi-tenant if Legacy co-invests.
- Morris County first (Rt 10 / Rt 46 / I-80: Randolph, Rockaway, Roxbury, Whippany, East Hanover, Livingston); Passaic, Bergen, Union acceptable.
- 1950s-70s buildings with heavy power (2,000A+), tailboard docks, drive-ins, divisible floor plates; vacancy is a plus, not a bar.
- Price $2M-$15M; sub-$100/SF basis on the solo deals to date.
- Land-rich sites (3.7-12.8 ac) with yard potential have traded.
- Lightly marketed or off-market through RRNNJ; all-cash close possible when Legacy is in.

## 8. Five questions for the first call
1. For 2026-2027 are you buying on your own balance sheet, through a numbered Commerce Park Ventures syndication, or with Legacy/Saddleback - and how much equity is uncommitted today?
2. Totowa went in and out in eight months; what is your intended hold on a Morris County small-bay deal versus a JV asset?
3. What occupancy floor do you need at closing, and would you take a fully vacant heavy-power building, a cold-storage conversion, or another yard-heavy site like Linden?
4. Which submarkets beyond Morris are on the 2026 list, and is Pennsylvania competing for the same equity?
5. Who signs off (Spencer, Roy, or a Legacy committee), how fast can you issue an LOI and close all-cash, and should new deals come through RRNNJ or direct?
