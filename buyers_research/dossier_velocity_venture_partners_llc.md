# Velocity Venture Partners, LLC - NJ industrial buyer dossier
*Prepared 2026-10-11 from buyers_research (A/B/C files) + 7 gap-filling searches. Confidence: med overall.*

## 1. Who they are and what they do in NJ
Velocity is a private, vertically integrated value-add industrial owner-operator (construction, leasing and management in-house), founded 2016-17 in Bala Cynwyd, PA by Tony Grelli and Zach Moore; no NJ office found in public sources (pack: https://www.velocityinv.com/contact-us; https://montco.today/2020/01/bala-cynwyd-based-velocity-venture-partners-continues-rapid-growth-with-acquisition-of-nj-industrial-property/). It claims 8M+ SF in the Northeast; equity source not disclosed, no SEC Form D located (pack). In NJ it has bought since 2019, only on the Philadelphia side: Pennsauken (it claims ~1.5M SF, the township's largest industrial landlord), Burlington and Mercer counties (https://re-nj.com/velocity-expands-footprint-in-pennsauken-with-purchase-of-560000-sq-ft-industrial-property/). The pattern: buy vacant or distressed older buildings at ~$57-62/SF, whitebox and demise them multi-tenant, then sell once stabilized - West Berlin ($4.2M, under three years after purchase) and Cherry Hill (https://www.citybiz.co/article/375629/velocity-venture-partners-sells-industrial-property-in-west-berlin-new-jersey/; https://www.binswanger.com/binswanger-helps-velocity-venture-partners-exit-stabilized-industrial-property-in-cherry-hill-nj/).

- Published range: "$1 - 50 million (ability to go significantly higher for specific opportunities)"; geography "Greater Northeast Region of the United States"; product "industrial & conversion-to-industrial (office/lab/retail to industrial)"; "deal feedback within 24 hours" (https://www.velocityinv.com/focus).
- A-row quote: "established in 2016 as a value-add industrial acquisition company" (pack, montco.today 2020-01, URL above).

## 4. Typical check size and deal shape
Priced NJ deals run $4.65M-$32M and 75K-560K SF, clustered at ~$57-62/SF - the pack notes Lawrenceville was about one-third of replacement cost. PA buys of $2M-$30.7M sit inside the published $1-50M band. All deals are single-asset (the Marlton and Medford parks are multi-building single sites); no JV, sale-leaseback or 1031 is disclosed and the capital stack is not found in public sources. Speed signals: Lawrenceville sourced direct with no broker, 9000 River Rd won at a bankruptcy auction, and a stated 24-hour read on new deals.

## 7. What would make a property match them
- Size: 60K-600K SF single building or multi-building flex park; sweet spot 75K-200K SF.
- Location: Philadelphia-side NJ - Pennsauken / Camden Co., Burlington Co. on Rt 130 and Turnpike Exits 4-6, Mercer Co. on Rt 1. No Northern or Central NJ buys seen.
- Condition: vacant, under-leased or obsolete; former manufacturing, flex, or office / lab / retail convertible to industrial. Not a stabilized Class A buyer.
- Price: $1M-$50M, historically ~$55-65/SF before capex; underwrites whitebox plus multi-tenant demising.
- Process: direct-from-owner and auction deals welcome; claims 24-hour feedback; will carry full vacancy.
- Also an exit candidate: sells NJ assets once leased (West Berlin, Cherry Hill).

## 8. Five questions for the first call
1. Is 2026 capital a discretionary fund or deal-by-deal equity, how much is uncommitted, and what hold period do you underwrite after the West Berlin and Cherry Hill exits?
2. Will you still take 100% vacancy (as at Lawrenceville) in 2026, and do outdoor storage / truck yards, cold storage or office-to-industrial conversions qualify in NJ?
3. Which NJ submarkets are on the 2026 list - still Camden / Burlington / Mercer, or anything north of Exit 7A, around 8A, or at the Port?
4. Who signs off after Will Riter's team, how fast can you go hard and close, and what killed the NJ deals you passed on in the last year?
5. Where do your NJ deals come from today - direct to owner (Aurobindo), trustees and auctions, or brokers - and which NJ brokers are you already working with?
